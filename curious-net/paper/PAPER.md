# Learning what affects what: a self-learning network with exact parity at zero experience (working draft)

*Status: living draft, regenerated and extended by the research loop. Numbers come from `results/*.json`; figures from `experiments/make_figures.py`. Every claim carries a ledger id (LEDGER.md) and every external statement a citation status (LITERATURE.md).*

## Abstract (provisional)
On a generator of random sparse dynamical systems with permuted, rescaled variables and up to 200 distractors, knowing which inputs are useful cuts an online MLP's first-1000-tick error from 0.84 to 0.40 and a transformer's from 0.79 to 0.50 (C9). A small learned per-input gate, fitted on the network's own permutation-importance labels from about ten earlier worlds, recovers a sizeable part of that benefit (MLP +0.128 +- 0.012 at K = 100, oracle-mask +0.209), saturates by K = 10, transfers under mild shifts of the generator (C10, C11, C14), and does not collapse when retrained on its own labels over 150 generations because each generation trains a fresh network on fresh real data (C13; the known result of Gerstgrasser et al. 2024 [unverified]). It does not learn causal structure: ground-truth labels train a worse gate than own labels, and its AUROC for true parents is 0.56-0.73 (C23, C29). Under Adam the gate acts as a learned per-input learning rate. Zero-experience parity with the plain network holds by construction (C12). The project's strongest results are negative or limiting: curiosity did not beat random designs for independent immediate effects (C3), a quantum-state encoding helps only on data with quantum-like structure (C16), structured randomness did not beat a single prior (C17), and detecting that a law lies outside the hypothesis library is limited by noise relative to the library's flexibility (C19). The hard oracle mask used as the reference in earlier drafts is not an upper bound (it is beaten in two settings), so gains are reported in absolute terms. All results are from a one-day sprint on one machine and have not been replicated.

## 1. Problem and motivation
Learners facing a new problem with many irrelevant inputs waste samples fitting them. If a learner has seen related problems it could carry over *structural* knowledge (which kinds of inputs matter) rather than weights. We test that with a design whose only experience-dependent component starts neutral, so improvements are attributable to experience.

## 3. Methods
**Worlds** (`src/worlds.py`): sparse random dynamical systems (kinds: lag, integrator, tanh, product, copy), 4-10 core variables, 1-3 actions, 10-200 distractors (autoregressive, sinusoid, white noise, noisy copies), noise 0.02-0.2, random permutation/scale/offset per scenario, known ground-truth graph used only for evaluation.
**Base learners**: online per-output MLPs (`src/nn.py`) and a variable-token transformer (`src/tfm.py`), Adam, batch size 1, online input/output standardisation; hyperparameters tuned on separate validation worlds.
**Self-learning component** (`src/selfnn.py`): eight scenario-independent pair descriptors measured on the first 100 ticks (linear/rank association, association of the input's last change, association after removing the output's own state, binned mutual information, input autocorrelation, self flag, action flag) -> relevance prior (ridge logistic or 2x32 tanh network) -> soft gate (input scaling for MLP; additive attention bias log p for the transformer). Labels: permutation importance of the agent's own trained network, paired z-test at Bonferroni level 1/n_inputs; ground-truth labels only as an upper-bound arm.
**Parity**: prior = none -> gate = 1 -> identical computation.

## 4. Results (figures: results/figures/)
See `figures/` and tables below; regenerated by `make_figures.py`.
- Headroom (exp K, N1): oracle input knowledge reduces first-1000-tick error from 0.84 to 0.40 (MLP, 200 distractors) and 0.794 to 0.500 (transformer, mixed worlds, +0.294 +- 0.024); the benefit grows with distractors (transformer: +0.218 at 10, +0.418 at 200).
- Experience curve (exp M, N1): see table in LOG.md; MLP prior gains +0.087 (K=3) .. +0.116 (K=300) (oracle +0.209); transformer prior +0.133..+0.149 for K>=10 (oracle +0.294), +0.177 with ground-truth labels at K>=100; K=1,3 noisy.
- Recursive generations (exp N2, 8 chains x 150 scenarios): gain over the plain transformer +0.142 (generation 5) -> +0.164 (20) -> +0.166 (40, 80, 150); no collapse; equal to the non-recursive prior at equal K on the same 24 worlds (+0.161..+0.167); plateau ~49% of the oracle-mask gain (+0.335) on those worlds; label positive rate stable at ~0.11. Mechanism of stability: labels come from fresh real data each generation; see ledger C13 for the limits.
- Earlier count-table results (sleep, lifelong curriculum, drag example): LOG.md; parked.

- FAIR baseline (R3, iteration 13; 48 worlds): a SOFT screen on one correlation statistic with two constants tuned on validation worlds gives +0.082 +- 0.008 vs the learned 2x32 prior +0.117 +- 0.011 (paired difference +0.035 +- 0.005); zero-constant soft p-value screen +0.055; hard screen +0.022. Most of the gate's benefit is soft gating of a calibrated statistic; cross-problem experience adds a modest, significant part (C30).
- Baselines (exp P, P2; MLP framework, 48 worlds; superseded on the no-experience side by R3): fixed correlation screen +0.009, input dropout -0.007, group-lasso (wide-grid tuned) +0.015, learned prior with hand-built descriptors +0.128 (61% of the oracle-mask gain +0.209), learned prior from a 1D CNN over raw pair windows +0.110-0.112 (52-54%). Learned structure priors, from either design, recover roughly half or more of the oracle gain; no-experience regularisers recover at most 7%.
- Out-of-family (exp Q): priors trained on the standard distribution and applied unchanged to families with different kinds, 4-6 parents, noise 0.4, copy-only distractors, and one-step-delayed effects keep 37-62% of the oracle-mask gain (control 63%/52%), never hurt, whereas a fixed correlation screen goes negative with copy-only distractors. The shifts are mild (same generator code, other settings).
- Where the recursive loop breaks (exp O, 5 chains x 60 generations per variant): labels from self-simulated data do not collapse the prior (+0.099 -> +0.135, real-label reference +0.133) because each generation's network is retrained from real data; a hard mask makes the loop decay (+0.063 -> +0.049, positive-label rate halves) because excluded inputs cannot be re-measured; 20% label noise caps the gain at +0.047 without compounding.

## 4b. Oracle-taught student: calibration and knowing its edge (E1/E2, iteration 14)
A relevance prior trained on ground-truth parent labels is well calibrated in its taught family (ECE 0.007, AUROC 0.90) and under mild shifts, under-predicts parenthood when worlds have more parents than taught, and is confidently wrong on real-connectome worlds (mean predicted 0.013 vs actual 0.236, AUROC 0.48). Its own uncertainty (ensemble disagreement, entropy) does not flag those worlds and is inverted there; distance of the world's statistics from the taught data flags most harmful shifts (AUROC 0.71-0.86) but misses fly-like worlds (0.46). Marginal split-conformal sets fail under shift and, for a 2% base-rate event, cover almost none of the positives even in-distribution (C31). This matches the general results that calibration is distribution-relative and that no detector catches every shift.

## 4c. Student-run experiments in law worlds (R6/X1, iteration 15)
With perfect measurement, five self-chosen drops identify the law family (100%), flag laws outside the library (100%, 0% false flags) and judge claims off by 1% correctly; experiment design does not matter. Under 3% noise the resolution limit returns and a discrimination-driven design helps early identification (C32).

## 4d. Energy as a constraint (B11, iteration 31)
An L1 wiring cost (lam 0.1, fixed in advance) cut shifted-situation error by 12.8% +- 2.7 and in-distribution error by 7.9% +- 1.1 on 48 fresh worlds while keeping 5% of the weight mass and halving activity (pre-registered replication, C41). An activity (spike) cost saved energy but only hurt. Figure `fig_energy.png`.

## 5. Limits and threats to validity
Single generator family (exp Q shifts are mild: same code, other settings); few seeds in some cells; one prior fit per K; between-experiment replication gap (exp N +0.156 vs exp O +0.128 at generation 10, same protocol) larger than the quoted within-experiment SEs; the no-experience baselines were weak (a hard Bonferroni screen; the fair soft screen is queued as R3); meta-learned neural baselines (in-context model, Reptile) not run; own labels come from a z-test on autocorrelated ticks; the hard oracle reference is not an upper bound; the Q12 'curious at n = 3' advantage is an artefact and the claim checker is given the generator's noise level; the constants audit fails for the self-learning line (76 undeclared literals). See `paper/FABLE_REVIEW.md`.

## 7. Biological grounding: how the generator's structure compares with real wiring (added 2026-10-22)
Data: FlyWire whole-brain connectome, proofread connections v783 (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10676866, CC BY 4.0): 138,639 neurons, 15.09M neuron pairs, 54.49M synapses. Using the Codex convention (a pair is connected if it has >= 5 synapses): median in-degree 9, mean 19.5, 99th percentile 167, maximum 5,080; 21.9% of neurons have in-degree <= 3. Weighting by synapse count, half of a neuron's incoming synapses come from a median of 3 partners (52% of neurons <= 3), 90% from a median of 9 (only 15% <= 3). The top 1% of neurons hold 21% of in-synapses; 14% of connections are reciprocal. Synapse mass: ACh 53%, GABA 25%, glutamate 21%.
Reading: the generator's 1-3 parents corresponds to the dominant inputs; the real long tail is heavier, so weak-but-real inputs would be hard to learn from few samples; relative to the number of possible inputs, real wiring is far sparser than any generator world. Test with a fly-like generator (exp R, median 8 inputs per output, power-law strengths, 48 worlds): knowing all true inputs +0.207, only the three strongest +0.160 (77%), learned prior +0.132 (64% of the all-inputs gain; 61% in standard worlds), in-family training +0.133, fixed screen +0.033. The learned prior's benefit survives fly-like in-degree and strength statistics; the generator does not reproduce hubs, recurrence, motifs or real dynamics. Next: connectome-derived worlds (Q19).
