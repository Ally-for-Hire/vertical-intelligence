# From correlation to principles: the proof ladder (thought experiment)

Read this if: you are deciding what to build toward the author's end goal (a model given only pre-quantum science and measurements that proposes quantum theory must exist). Source: the author, 2026-10-28 ([[../raw/2026-10-28-correlation-to-principles]]). Not an experiment yet; a map of which rungs are proven, which are not, and what each unproven rung must show BEFORE it is allowed into the model (standing rule 'earn every component', [[research-protocol]] s5).

Five-line summary: the target is a model that, given variables that are correlated but not obviously related, finds the dependence AND the underlying law, not only the correlation. Four of the needed abilities have partial evidence in toys; two have none. Every rung is tested alone against a fair baseline before it is combined. The register of usable pieces is curious-net `paper/PROVEN_PIECES.md`. The benchmark repair (audit R-V7) and X1.4 come first because the later rungs depend on trustworthy comparisons and on a trustworthy boredom/learning signal.

## The ladder
| Rung | Ability | Evidence today | What it must show before use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notice that two or more signals depend on each other even when the link is nonlinear and indirect (not just a correlation coefficient) | C30/C33 relevance gate (a learned screen; KEEP, but it is a learned learning rate, not causal) | detection power vs a tuned dependence test (mutual information / HSIC) on planted nonlinear and indirect links, with a null world; false-positive rate |
| 2 | Tell correlation from cause: a hidden common cause, a chain, a direct link | law worlds: perfect measurement makes design irrelevant in 1-D (C32); causal worlds with interventions not done | the student runs its own interventions; recovers the planted graph; beats a non-interventional baseline given the same data budget |
| 3 | Find the functional form: which equation relates them | E9c: a 6-model library containing the truth, selected by BIC (toy; the library contains the law by construction) | recover forms NOT in the library by search (symbolic regression) with a complexity penalty; false-law rate on null data |
| 4 | Say what the model cannot explain: reject bad theories, flag a failing law | C31 calibration, E2 novelty (partial: input distance works, own uncertainty fails); E9 hole-poking (toy) | flags a planted anomaly without false alarms at a fixed rate |
| 5 | Invent a missing term or constant that the known library lacks (a Planck-like quantum) | none (Q12d, I3 toy, Q-PRE1900 all untested) | on synthetic data from a held-out law family, proposes a new constant and recovers its value without being told; baseline = refit with a free constant |
| 6 | Propose a principle (for example quantisation or a conserved quantity) rather than a formula | none | the hardest rung; defined only after rung 5 passes |

Prediction-only life (X1.3) and the hidden-need worlds supply the DRIVE for rungs 1-5 (curiosity that stays at the edge of learning, boredom as a guard against the dark room), not the abilities themselves. X1.4 tests whether that drive survives a realistic (noisy, lagged) boredom signal, which any later rung will have.

## Rules for climbing
1. A rung is entered only with a registered test that includes a planted null (no relation) and a fair baseline with equal tuning.
2. A passed rung is recorded in `PROVEN_PIECES.md` with its audit status; combinations are tested against their parts.
3. Honest limit, stated up front: toy worlds prove principles in miniature; the final exam (Q-PRE1900) uses real historical data (blackbody, Balmer lines, photoelectric effect) and is the only test of the end goal.
