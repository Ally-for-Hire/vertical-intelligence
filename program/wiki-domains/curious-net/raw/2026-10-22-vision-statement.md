# Curious-net: the user's end-goal statement (2026-10-22, from voice dictation, condensed but close to the original)

General idea: in understanding the world it continuously improves at understanding how best to affect its environment, making a more and more efficient neural network (not necessarily in time or compute, but in GENERATION COUNT). Every new applied problem gives it a faster understanding of how to affect the next problem. End goal: a LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL that applies everything we know and uses this algorithm to RECURSIVELY SELF-IMPROVE. Ideally with internet access, self-research and self-expansion "until it has no threads to pull on". The user explains abstract concepts to it; it connects them to previously learned and discussed things, understands them completely and immediately, and applies them to future problems.

Right now: test the concept of a TRANSFORMER model that (somewhat necessarily) recursively self-improves. "How much faster in neural-network terms when it already knows which inputs matter" is vital.

Example: "when I drop a ball it hits the ground in 5 seconds, say." The model should put it in its brain, connect it with physics/gravity/the world, or understand that it cannot explain it and must research physics until it can, then interpret it. Then: "if I drop a ball from 15 m, how long does it take to fall?" The traditional transformer predicts the next word; this one should first understand the situation in its entirety and then apply that understanding to interpret and explain the answer.

Extremely large project; right now independent functions are tested; the final product is imagined as a literal physical brain realisable in hardware, self-scaling on the same concepts as biology and psychology.

The advantage of current AI is not correctness but the ability to throw the whole human library at a task. Goal: an AI that, given understanding of physics, discovers quantum theory: understands its own limits of understanding, researches and experiments in its own way, and discovers a theorem, because the theorem exists in the universe and can be derived from attainable knowledge. An algorithm that knows what knowledge to attain and how it can be applied, that continuously learns, given monstrous amounts of data, generations and experience.

Method idea: self-run pre-existing LLMs that monitor it offline and teach it with minimal human involvement so the user can "just sit and let it grind". Then talk to it about various things; the AI asks it random questions; it understands everything and answers. Hypothetically it should never be wrong: given enough data, its own understanding or lack of it, it should know when it is going to give a wrong answer, that what it says is not the complete picture, that it does not have all the variables.

It should be implicitly both curious and skeptical: take every input as subjective, something to be checked against all previous inputs; if it cannot find the connection, it wants to learn more.
