# The Dream (capital D), zoomed out

Written 2026-10-22 from the user's own words across both research sessions: the NeuroBlock session ("hi", Oct 18-19, `~/neuroblock-research/FOUNDATIONS.md`) and the curious-net session (Oct 22). **The user's statements are the givens. Everything labelled "proposed" or "assessment" is the assistant's.**

## 1. The Dream

**A better transformer: a new kind of AI model that changes how AI operates.** It is built from what we currently understand about **biology**, **psychology**, and what **intelligence, knowledge and wisdom** are. Everything done so far is small experiments that test pieces of it.

The proof the Dream has been reached (user, Oct 22): a language model that is given only the science and measurements from before quantum physics, is never told quantum theory exists, and proposes that it must exist. More broadly, a model that **can actually learn and create semi-new things**.

## 2. The three foundations, in the user's words

### Biology: a brain is a physical, energy-limited machine made of parts
- The brain **converts the body's senses into complex, abstract understanding and lowers uncertainty in its environment.**
- It is **split into parts** (at least five, like the human brain). The named ones are:
  - an **emotional converter** (inputs to emotions)
  - an **intellectual converter** (inputs to context graphs that can be followed)
  - **sensors**
  - the **Self**, which decides what to do and how to act
- **Building the Self is the main focus.** The Self has no parts; parts and sub-parts are generated on demand as situations need more context.
- **Energy is foundational**: nothing is free, efficiency is vital, and the brain should minimise energy naturally. Overload forces shutdown, and fatigue lowers the threshold.
- **Sleep**: the brain chooses when to sleep, to strengthen connections, and has to sleep if it does too much work.
- **"Life is better at making brains than humans are."** Start from the human brain, the only working example. The future of AI may be a brain scan precise enough to code, then optimise; the fruit fly is the stepping stone.

### Psychology: how minds actually get good
- **Genius = motivation + processing speed, motivation being the big one.** Work from what works in humans.
- The IFS view of the mind: a central Self plus parts.
- **Mastery**: understand a problem so intrinsically that doing it again costs zero energy.
- The system is **curious and skeptical**. It treats every input as a claim to check against what it already understands, and flags what it cannot connect.
- It is taught from **literally zero** (not even human language) by an offline LLM acting as **parent and teacher**. The oracle teaches its own limits, or teaches in a way that exposes them. The student must be able to **run its own experiments**.

### Intelligence, knowledge, wisdom
- **Intelligence**: getting better *at taking information and producing more information*, not at specific problems. Every new problem is solved faster, with less energy, by connecting it to every previous problem.
- **Knowledge**: *the universe is built on foundational blocks and everything is connected to them; all problems intrinsically relate.* Store as little as possible, and reuse it, because reuse is the most energy-efficient thing.
- **Wisdom**: knowing what it does not understand, pushing at that boundary, and not being confidently wrong ("never wrong" reframed as calibrated).
- **Purpose**: a learning criterion, "a de facto meaning of life", and with it the stepping stones to consciousness.

## 4. Assessment: what has drifted, and what to bring back

1. **curious-net narrowed to statistics.** It asked "which input matters" on small simulated worlds. That is a real ingredient (knowledge as reusable structure), but it lost the brain: energy, parts, the Self, sleep, purpose.
2. **The two sessions answer different halves.** NeuroBlock built a living, energy-limited brain but never tested generality across problems. Curious-net tested generality and skepticism, but on a brain-less learner. The Dream needs both in one system.
3. **The Self, the user's main focus, has had zero experiments.**
4. **No transformer work beyond one gate.** If the Dream is a better transformer, each biological or psychological principle should eventually become a concrete change to how a transformer computes, learns or remembers. Candidates (proposed):
   - energy as a compute budget per token (adaptive computation)
   - sleep as offline consolidation of a reusable library
   - foundational blocks as a shared, growing library of reusable sub-programs
   - parts generated on demand as sparse, spawned experts
   - the Self as a controller that allocates attention and energy by learning progress
   - calibrated abstention plus experiments as the wisdom layer
5. **The pre-quantum test is the right final exam.** It requires all the pillars together: knowledge (classical physics as blocks), intelligence (producing new information from it), wisdom (noticing the classical failure is real), and an ability to invent (a new constant / quantisation).

## 5. Proposed next arc (not agreed; for the user to steer)

1. **Re-unite the two lines.** One brain that is energy-limited, sleeps to consolidate, keeps a small library of reusable blocks, runs its own experiments, and knows its limits, tested across many problems from a made-up universe of runes with hidden foundational blocks. This is the test of "all problems relate".
2. **The Self v0.** A controller whose only job is choosing what to do next (explore, exploit, sleep, experiment, ask the teacher), driven by learning progress per unit of energy. That is the measurable version of motivation and purpose.
3. **Inventing, not just choosing.** Library learning in the DreamCoder style plus sparse symbolic regression, so a missing law can be proposed. Then the pre-1900 benchmark: toy first, real historical data next.
4. **Only then, transformer translation.** Each principle that survives a fair test becomes a proposed transformer modification, tested against a plain transformer with exact parity at zero experience, as the project already does.

## 6. Limits worth keeping in view (from both sessions)

- Far transfer between unrelated skills is weak in humans and in machines. Transfer works when structure is shared, which is exactly what the "foundational blocks" belief claims. The made-up rune universe is how to test it fairly.
- Learning-to-learn plateaus once a family of problems is learned; open-ended growth needs ever-new problem families, so it is a property of the curriculum and the teacher, not only of the learner.
- No detector catches every kind of novelty. Wisdom is calibrated honesty inside what was learned plus experiments at the boundary, not infallibility.
- Training on one's own output adds no information about the world; fresh data or experiments are required.
