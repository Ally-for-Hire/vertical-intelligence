Status: **[verified]** = a search in this project returned a source that states the point (details of the original paper not necessarily read); **[derived]** = follows from a verified general result; **[unverified]** = from memory, queued for checking. ## Verified so far

| topic | reference | what it says, as far as verified | what it means for this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tracking changing environments | Herbster & Warmuth, "Tracking the best expert" (1998); the fixed-share algorithm mixes each expert's updated weight with a uniform share via a switching parameter; regret is measured against the best segment-wise sequence of experts [verified via secondary sources; original theorems not read] | the context/variant posterior in core4 and the trust statistic follow this family; it gives the regret bound for "recognise a regime change quickly" |
| Universal coding of binary sources | Krichevsky & Trofimov (1981); minimax redundancy for k-symbol memoryless sources is (k-1)/2 log n + O(1); KT = Dirichlet(1/2,...) [verified for the general k formula; binary case (1/2) log n [derived]] | the "1/2 log n bits per parameter" used in every MDL gate; why plug-in gains over-reward sparse cells (found in core3 debugging) |
| Meta-learning as Bayesian inference | Ortega et al., "Meta-learning of Sequential Strategies" (2019, arXiv:1905.03030): memory-based meta-learned strategies are near-optimal because they amortise Bayes-filtered data [verified] | in-distribution, a network trained across tasks adapts like a Bayes-optimal agent: the strongest baseline for the project's "gets faster with experience" claim |
| Meta-trained agents | Mikulik et al., NeurIPS 2020: meta-trained agents behave like Bayes-optimal agents, with a similar computational structure; Bayes-optimal agents are fixed points of meta-learning dynamics [verified] | supports expecting NN meta-learners to match the wrapper in-distribution |
| Neural causal discovery | Ke et al., "Learning Neural Causal Models from Unknown Interventions" (arXiv:1910.01075): continuous optimisation + neural nets, observational and interventional data, unknown intervention targets, meta-learned structure [verified] | the closest published neighbour of the relevance prior; must be cited and ideally compared |
| Training on own outputs | Shumailov et al., "AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data", Nature 631, 755-759 (2024): recursive training on generated data loses distribution tails; shown for VAEs, GMMs, LLMs [verified via summaries; full text not read] | the main risk for the recursive loop (C13): labels produced by the model's own networks can compound errors |
| Experimental design / active learning | Houlsby et al., BALD (2011, arXiv:1112.5745): choose points where posterior parameter samples disagree; mutual information between label and parameters = predictive entropy minus expected entropy [verified] | the information-gain curiosity used in the project is BALD-like; its known behaviour (helps most when evidence is costly) frames C3 |
| Calibration | Guo et al., "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017: modern nets are poorly calibrated; temperature scaling is a one-parameter fix; ECE measures the confidence-accuracy gap (exact binning not confirmed) [verified] | the testable form of "knows when it is wrong" (C15) |


## Verified in loop iteration 1 (2026-10-22; abstracts/summaries, full texts not read)

| topic | reference | what it says, as far as verified | what it means here |

| MDL | Rissanen, "Modeling by Shortest Data Description", Automatica 14(5):465-471 (1978): the best model gives the shortest description of the data; yields estimates of integer-valued structure parameters and real-valued parameters; related to BIC [verified] | the project's "add a connection only if it saves more bits than it costs" is the MDL criterion; Rissanen 1978 is the foundation cited |
| First-order meta-learning | Nichol, Achiam, Schulman, "On First-Order Meta-Learning Algorithms" (arXiv:1803.02999): Reptile samples a task, trains on it, moves the initialisation toward the trained weights; first-order MAML ignores second derivatives [verified]. MAML itself (Finn, Abbeel, Levine, ICML 2017) was NOT returned by this search: stays [unverified] | Reptile is the right "meta-learned initialisation" baseline (Q2e); it needs a fixed input layout, which the generator deliberately breaks by permuting variables |
| Complementary learning systems | McClelland, McNaughton, O'Reilly, Psychological Review 102(3):419-457 (1995): hippocampus stores new memories fast and replays them to the neocortex, which learns slowly and interleaved to extract structure; a 2011 review found the framework largely supported [verified] | the two-timescale (fast counts / permanent structure) design and the user's "sleep" idea are the CLS proposal in algorithmic form; parked |
| Curiosity as compression progress | Schmidhuber, "Formal Theory of Creativity, Fun, and Intrinsic Motivation (1990-2010)", IEEE Trans. Autonomous Mental Development (2010): intrinsic reward for finding patterns that improve prediction/compression; a predictor module plus a reinforcement learner that chooses actions to maximise the predictor's improvement [verified at abstract level] | the learning-progress reward proposed for the experiment policy (Q6) is this; its noisy-TV safety comes from rewarding improvement, not surprise |
| Equation discovery from data | Brunton, Proctor, Kutz, "Discovering governing equations from data by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamical systems" (SINDy), PNAS 113(15):3932-3937 (2016): sparse regression over candidate nonlinear terms; extended to control (SINDYc) [verified] | baseline for law-discovery tests (Q12): the free-fall law is a two-term sparse model |
| Symbolic regression | Udrescu & Tegmark, "AI Feynman", Science Advances (2020): recovers all 100 Feynman-lecture equations (previous public software 71); hard set 15% -> 90%; combines neural fitting with physics-inspired decomposition (symmetry, separability) [verified] | the state of the art on "derive a law from data"; a fair target for what discovery claims must beat |
| Library learning with wake-sleep | Ellis et al., "DreamCoder", PLDI 2021: learns a library of reusable program components and a neural search policy in a wake-sleep loop; solves more problems faster; domains include equation discovery [verified] | the closest published relative of "concept units + sleep consolidation" and of "each solved problem makes the next faster" |
| Self-training loops | Zelikman et al., "STaR: Bootstrapping Reasoning With Reasoning", NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv:2203.14465): generate rationales, keep those that lead to correct answers (retry with the answer as a hint otherwise), fine-tune, repeat; beats fine-tuning to predict answers directly on several sets [verified at abstract level] | a self-improvement loop that works, and it works because a VERIFIER (the correct answer) filters what the model learns from; contrast with model collapse (Shumailov 2024) where no such filter exists. For C13 the question is what plays the verifier role for our own-label loop |

## Connectomics and brain emulation (verified in loop iteration 1b; abstracts/summaries/press, full texts not read)

| topic | reference | what it says, as far as verified | what it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-brain fly connectome | Dorkenwald, Schlegel et al. and the FlyWire consortium, Nature (Oct 2024, nine-paper package): wiring diagram of an adult female Drosophila brain, 139,255 neurons and about 5x10^7 chemical synapses (press figure ~54.5M); EM images from 2018, machine reconstruction plus human proofreading; annotations of cell classes/types and predicted neurotransmitters [verified via secondary reports; the main abstract itself was not retrieved]. Counts differ by release and definition (e.g. 127,978 proofread neurons, 53M synapses in a Codex snapshot; Codex counts a pair as connected at >= 5 synapses) | the scan the user wants to "get a jump" on exists for a fly; its public connectivity data is the natural real-world test bed for the project's structure-recovery claims |
| Network statistics of that connectome | Lin, Yang, Dorkenwald et al. (companion paper): rich-club organisation, ~30% of the connectome highly connected [verified via a secondary summary] | real wiring is sparse but heavy-tailed with hubs; the generator's "1-3 parents per output" is a simplification to test (C22) |
| Central-brain connectome (hemibrain) | Scheffer et al., eLife 9:e57443 (2020): central brain, ~25,000 neurons, ~20M connections, cell types and atlas [verified via summary] | an independent reconstruction for cross-checking statistics |
| Connectome-based whole-brain dynamics | Shiu et al. (2024; bioRxiv 2023, PMC11446845): leaky integrate-and-fire model of the whole central brain from synapse counts and predicted neurotransmitters; activating taste neurons predicts neurons required for feeding; motor-neuron predictions validated optogenetically [verified at abstract level; venue confirmed iteration 13: Nature 634, 210-219 (2024), doi 10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9]. Limits noted by the authors' setup: no gap junctions, no neuromodulation beyond excitation/inhibition, zero basal firing | wiring + simple dynamics already predicts some circuit function: partial support for "code the scan and optimise" (C21) |
| Connectome-constrained learning | Lappalainen et al., Nature 634:1132-1140 (2024): network built from measured connectivity of 64 cell types in the optic-lobe motion pathway; unknown single-neuron/synapse parameters fitted by deep learning on a motion task; predictions agree with experiments from 26 studies; works better when neurons are sparsely connected [verified] | the clearest published instance of "structure from the scan, parameters by optimisation"; its sparsity finding is the biological counterpart of this project's sparse-structure assumption and of the headroom result (C9) |
| Whole-brain emulation | Sandberg & Bostrom, "Whole Brain Emulation: A Roadmap", FHI Technical Report 2008-3 (~130 pp.), defines emulation vs simulation and success criteria [verified]; Zanichelli, Schons, Freeman, Shiu & Arkhipov, "State of Brain Emulation Report 2025", arXiv:2510.15745 (Oct 2025; identifier re-confirmed iteration 13): reassesses progress in recording neural dynamics, mapping structure (connectomics), and emulation/embodiment [abstract verified; the report's specific findings were not retrieved] | the field's own state-of-play; the project should cite it for what is and is not missing |
| Limits of the connectome alone | Sources agree: gap junctions are hard to detect and often absent from models; neuromodulation is largely missing; synaptic weights are modelling choices (synapse count vs relative fraction vs size changed accuracy in a larval motor-circuit study); predictions from different fits can disagree; a bioRxiv preprint ("Infrequent strong connections constrain connectomic predictions of neuronal function", 2025) and a conference talk argue wiring predicts function less well than hoped [titles and abstracts only; content not examined; treat as a debated point] | "scan precisely enough to code a brain" needs more than wiring: weights, dynamics, neuromodulators, plasticity; this is where a learner of what-affects-what and of parameters from activity (the project) is needed |
| Data availability | FlyWire connectivity data, Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.10676866 (v783, 2024-06-02, CC BY 4.0, 10.6 GB total; `proofread_connections_783.feather` 852 MB = synapse counts and mean neurotransmitter probabilities per proofread neuron pair and neuropil; `proofread_root_ids_783.npy` 1.1 MB; the full synapse table is 9.5 GB) [verified from the record page] | a bounded test is possible with the 852 MB table |

## Teacher-parent and developmental training from zero (verified in loop iteration 1c; abstracts/summaries, full texts not read)

| topic | reference | what it says, as far as verified | what it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developmentally plausible data budgets | Warstadt et al., BabyLM Challenge (CoNLL 2023; arXiv:2301.11796): sample-efficient pretraining on 10M / 100M word corpora; motivation: children acquire language from < 100M words while LLMs typically use 3-4 orders of magnitude more [verified] | the yardstick for "a student that learns language from a small, structured, teacher-chosen stream" |
| Teacher-written curriculum | Eldan & Li, "TinyStories" (arXiv:2305.07759, 2023): a synthetic corpus of short stories using only words a 3-4-year-old knows, generated by GPT-3.5/GPT-4; models below 10M parameters (even one transformer block) produce fluent, consistent multi-paragraph stories; GPT-4 used as a grader [verified at abstract level] | an existing, successful case of an LLM teacher producing a simple curriculum for a tiny student; note it is one-shot data generation, not an interactive, adaptive loop, and the student starts from text, not from nothing |
| Grounded language learning from minimal prior knowledge | Hermann et al., "Grounded Language Learning in a Simulated 3D World" (arXiv:1706.06551, 2017): an agent with minimal prior knowledge learns to link words to perception and action from reward; generalises to new instructions; learns new words faster as its semantic knowledge grows [verified at abstract level] | closest published analogue of "start from zero, learn language by acting in a world"; the acceleration with knowledge is the same effect as our experience curve |
| Interactive teaching platform | Chevalier-Boisvert et al., BabyAI (ICLR 2019, arXiv:1810.08272): 19 levels of a compositional synthetic language, a scripted bot simulating a human teacher, curriculum and interactive teaching; conclusion: current deep-learning methods are not yet sufficiently sample efficient for compositional language [verified] | a ready-made benchmark for teacher-student language learning and the honest baseline: it is hard |
| Emergent language between agents | Lazaridou, Peysakhovich, Baroni (ICLR 2017, arXiv:1612.07182): sender-receiver referential game; arbitrary symbols become a coordination code; grounding in natural language needs extra steps [verified] | shows a protolanguage can arise from interaction with no text; a candidate for the first stage (before any human language) |
| Optimal teaching | Zhu, "Machine Teaching: An Inverse Problem to Machine Learning and an Approach Toward Optimal Education", AAAI 2015: find the best training set for a given learner and target; the minimum set size is the teaching dimension (Goldman & Kearns 1995) [verified] | the theory of a teacher that models the student; adaptive teaching = teaching the current student's gaps (C25) |
| Cultural transmission of language | Kirby, Cornish, Smith, PNAS 105(31):10681-10686 (2008): artificial languages passed down chains of learners become easier to learn and more structured with no designer; the bottleneck (limited sample per generation) is central to the underlying models but not confirmed in the abstracts retrieved [verified; bottleneck detail unverified] | the positive counterpart of model collapse: a learner chain can increase structure; whether it does depends on the bottleneck and verification (C27) |
| Statistical learning in infants | Saffran, Aslin, Newport, Science 274:1926-1928 (1996): 8-month-olds segment words from a continuous stream using transitional probabilities after about 2 minutes of exposure; artificial-speech stimuli, generalisation later questioned [verified] | the pre-linguistic bootstrapping step is statistical: a count-based learner can implement it; 'literally zero' starts here |

## Quantum-like models of judgment (verified in loop iteration 11; abstracts/summaries/secondary reviews, full texts not read)

| topic | reference | what it says, as far as verified | what it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantum cognition | Busemeyer & Bruza, *Quantum Models of Cognition and Decision* (Cambridge University Press, 2012): quantum probability gives a coherent account of violations of the law of total probability and of question-order effects in judgment and decision studies; quantum logic need not obey the distributive law [verified via summaries and a review] | the strongest published case for quantum-style representations in a COGNITIVE setting: the claim is about human judgment data, not about learning algorithms or physical systems |
| The QQ equality | Wang & Busemeyer, "A Quantum Question Order Model Supported by Empirical Tests of an A Priori and Precise Prediction", Topics in Cognitive Science (2013), doi 10.1111/tops.12040: a parameter-free prediction for two yes/no questions asked in both orders, p(a0,b1)+p(a1,b0) = p(b0,a1)+p(b1,a0); tested on survey data with a good fit; a quantum model of order effects needs fewer parameters than a general classical order-dependent model [equality and test verified; the '70 surveys' figure was NOT confirmed] | the exact constraint used in the Q9 toy |
| Critiques | Lebedev & Khrennikov (arXiv:1811.00045; iteration 13: this identifier could NOT be re-confirmed by search, treat as unverified until the arXiv page is read): with POVM instead of projective measurements the QQ equality can be violated; a separate paper asks whether the equality is really a quantum test [verified at abstract level] | the QQ equality is a property of one quantum model class, not proof that cognition is quantum; classical models can also be built to satisfy it |

## Iteration 61: sources for CTX6/7 (context checking where memories disagree)

- Seung, Opper & Sompolinsky 1992, 'Query by committee', COLT 1992 [u: bibliographic only, from memory; a search did not surface the paper]. The idea: ask for labels where a committee of hypotheses disagrees. Our memory-disagreement trigger is a nearest-neighbour cousin of this. Not shown: anything about routing or specialists.
- Arazo et al. 2020, 'Pseudo-labeling and confirmation bias in deep semi-supervised learning', IJCNN 2020, arXiv:1908.02983 [v: abstract level]. Shows that a network trained on its own guessed labels fits its wrong guesses (confirmation bias), reduced by mixup and a minimum share of real labels per batch. Relevant to CTX7's surprise (confident wrong routing does damage). Not shown: routing, or that error-based assignment is harmless; our 'harmless misroutes' hypothesis is our own and untested.
